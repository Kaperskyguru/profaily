One dashboard says the video reached 40,000 views. Another reports 11,000 engaged views. A third
shows how many people saw the thumbnail in the feed and chose to watch. Those numbers are not
interchangeable.

Short-form video teams get better decisions when they stop asking for one cross-platform “winner”
and instead ask where a video earned distribution, held attention, prompted a response, or moved a
business goal.

## A view is not a universal unit {#views}

YouTube changed how Shorts views are counted on March 31, 2025: a view counts when a Short starts or
replays, with no minimum watch time. YouTube retained the previous metric under the name *engaged
views*, which shows how many viewers chose to continue watching. The change can make the top-line
view count rise without changing how the underlying video performs with viewers. [YouTube explains
the change in its Shorts help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en-GB).

YouTube Studio also surfaces Shorts-specific signals such as “shown in feed” and “how many chose to
view.” Its Analytics API defines measures including views, engaged views, average view duration,
shares, and audience-retention ratios. Those definitions belong to YouTube; another network may
define or expose its metrics differently. [See YouTube’s Shorts analytics
tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en) and
[the API metric definitions](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/metrics).

> **Reporting rule:** use each platform’s named metric, definition, and date range. Put the
> definitions next to the chart. A shared dashboard should standardize the question—not pretend the
> underlying counters are identical.

## Use four measurement layers {#layers}

Organize a review in the order a viewer and a business experience the video:

1. **Distribution:** Was the video shown or served? Record each platform’s available reach,
  impressions, or feed-distribution measure.
2. **Choice and watch quality:** Did someone stop, continue, and stay? Use the platform’s own
  view-selection and watch-time or retention metrics. Compare a video to similar videos on that
  platform.
3. **Audience response:** Did viewers save, share, comment, follow, or ask a useful question?
  Separate lightweight reactions from actions that show intent.
4. **Business outcome:** Did the video produce qualified conversations, leads, bookings, sales, or
  support outcomes? Count only outcomes your tracking can connect back with reasonable confidence.

Not every video should be judged on the last layer. A product tutorial may be designed to reduce
repeated support questions; a campaign video may be intended to create qualified demand. Write down
the job before you publish, then choose one primary signal and a few diagnostic signals.

## Compare like with like {#compare}

Use the same platform, format, audience, time window, and campaign intent when comparing videos. A
20-second product demonstration should not be ranked against a 90-second founder story simply
because both are vertical clips. If the question is the first three seconds, inspect where viewers
leave; if it is distribution, inspect where people found the video.

For a multi-platform campaign, keep a row per destination:

| Destination | Primary question | Platform-native signal | Business signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok | Did the video earn attention and meaningful response? | Use available TikTok analytics for the account | Qualified comments or tracked referral activity |
| Instagram | Did people watch, share, save, or start a conversation? | Use the account’s available Reels insights | Qualified inquiries or attributed actions |
| YouTube | Was it shown, chosen, and watched? | Shown in feed, chose to view, engaged views, watch signals | Subscribers, qualified inquiries, or tracked visits |
| Facebook | Did it reach the intended audience and prompt a response? | Use available video and audience insights | Qualified conversations and attributable outcomes |

The table is a reporting scaffold, not a promise that every metric is available for every account or
tool. Use the connected platform’s current analytics and permissions.

## Turn the report into the next test {#decide}

End every review with one decision: repeat, revise, or stop. If a video was served but viewers
swiped away, test the opening. If people watched but did not act, make the next step clearer. If a
video created valuable questions, make a follow-up that answers them. Change one meaningful variable
at a time so the team can tell what the experiment was.
